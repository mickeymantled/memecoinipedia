## 2. How many of the 500 are wrong, and in which direction

Measured by recomputing all 500 coins from stored bars under the rule changes of section 8 (`ingest/scripts/a6/proposals.ts`), and counting the review verdicts:

- ATH too high by more than 15%: **23 coins** (the liquidity floor lowers 21 of them by 20% to 99.7%; for ETH and Gorda it removes the ATH). Cause: drained pools.
- ATH too low by more than 15%: **15 coins** (14 where one-minute spikes or the five-trader rule hold the ATH down, plus XST, which has no ATH and should have about $2.1M).
- 2 coins are in both lists (a drained-pool coin that a lone spike also raises), so the distinct total is **36 of 500 (7.2%)**.
- All 23 coins whose ATH is wrong by review verdict are in the 36. 13 of the 36 are outside groups 1 to 6 (found only by the recompute, including 3 of the rugged sample), so coins that no group selected are not clean.
- This is a floor for the failure modes found here. I did not measure how many unselected coins are wrong in ways the variants do not detect, and the 4 undetermined coins are not in the count.

Status: **14 coins change listed status** under the variants: 10 listed coins would drop below $100k under the liquidity floor (all 10 are drained-pool coins in groups 1, 2 or 4 with $0 to $64 of liquidity at the ATH), and 4 unlisted coins would be listed (x373PX $84k to $116k, 2kHFGk $65k to $112k, 3Ercdc $8k to $204k, XST none to $2.12M).

The too-high direction does more damage to listing (10 false listings against 4 missed) because the drained coins have the largest ATHs ($6.5M, $1.4M, $626k).

## 3. Rugged sample (group 7, 20 coins)

By `docs/metrics.md` section 10, 20 of 20 coins stand as rugs by price: the close 30 minutes after the ATH is at 0% to 43% of the ATH for all of them with a bar, and the highest strict price after the crash is 0% to 44% of the ATH (a recovery to 50% would clear the flag). The crash came from a strict point for 18 coins and from a point that is not strict for 2 (USDP, Anthropic: pool liquidity $1.1M and $207k at the ATH, falling to $2.1k and $1.9k an hour later, price at 0% after 30 minutes). Rug flag judged wrong: **0 of 20** (no false positive where price came back).

Judged wrong on the ATH rather than the flag: **3 of 20 (15%), ATH too high.** BEAST (5FRhBQRg), USOV and ANTP set their ATH while the pool held $1,837, $1,482 and $1,981 of liquidity. With a $5,000 liquidity floor their ATHs fall from $672k to $470k (-30%), $431k to $357k (-17%) and $2.89M to $2.33M (-19%), and the rug flag is no longer set for BEAST and ANTP, because the crash no longer falls within 30 minutes of the lower ATH. The collapse itself is real for all three (price at 0% of the ATH 30 minutes later); what is in doubt is the ATH and the timing of the pattern.

Not determined: whether the rule misses rugs. The sample holds rugged coins only. Widening `rugWithinSec` from 1,800 s to 6 hours adds 115 rug flags to the 171 that stand (286 of 500); I did not inspect those 115.

## 8. Proposals, strongest evidence first

Nothing below is applied. All recomputes are over the 500 coins, from stored bars, with `ingest/scripts/a6/proposals.ts` (rules unchanged except for the named change).

1. **Liquidity floor for the ATH.** Rule: a 1-minute window can set the ATH only when the pool's liquidity at the window's last bar is at least $5,000. Rests on 14 drained-pool coins in groups 1, 2 and 4 (CABARA, BWULL, CHOWIE, ETH, DIDDY, AIvideo, Gorda, PENNY, UGLYASSKI, BUN, qtum, SEC, floppa, ORVILLE) and 3 rugged coins (BEAST, USOV, ANTP). Effect on the 500: 25 ATHs fall by more than 15% or disappear, 10 listed coins drop below $100k (DIDDY $6.48M to $17k, floppa $1.37M to $69k, ORVILLE $626k to $70k, SEC $203k to $99k, CHOWIE $183k to $48k, qtum $143k to $36k, AIvideo $127k to $88k, BWULL $118k to $44k, CABARA $116k to $54k, BUN $111k to $31k), 10 rug flags change. What it might break: small markets that never hold $5,000 (Kolwaii $5k to none, JM $6k to none, both under the listing line, and not drained: $4.1k and $4.2k of liquidity); the first minutes of a coin, before liquidity is built; the liquidity figure itself (it is the bar's reported liquidity, not checked against a pool state). A floor of $2,500 would separate drained pools (median $12, all $64 or less) from small ones better, but only $5,000 was tested.
2. **Let a lone bar set the ATH when it is large: at least $5,000 and 25 wallets in one minute.** Rests on FuVSxG, 2kHFGk, 5Tr7EC, CaNBzB, 9Vw663. Effect: 10 coins rise by more than 15% (445k to 571k, 65k to 112k, 145k to 203k, 21k to 54k, 50k to 66k, and five others), 2 coins cross $100k (2kHFGk, 3Ercdc), 5 rug flags change. What it might break: **apply it only with proposal 1.** On its own it raises 2 drained-pool coins (CABARA $116k to $214k, DIDDY $6.48M to $110M) and 3 thin-pool coins (3Ercdc $8k to $204k at $5.6k liquidity, Kolwaii $5k to $86k, JM $6k to $65k) where one buy moves a thin pool. The evidence is weaker than for proposal 1: none of the 10 could be checked against a third source (all older than 180 days).
3. **Five-trader rule to three wallets.** Rests on XST, x373PX, FShevY, iRT2MT, GOP. Effect: 4 coins rise by more than 15% (283k to 330k, 84k to 116k, 123k to 173k, 80k to 98k), XST gets an ATH of $2.12M; 2 coins cross $100k (x373PX, XST). What it might break: three wallets can be one actor. XST's price is corroborated by Dune and the daily bar; the other four have no third source.
4. **Two consecutive points before the level falls** (the section 13 candidate). Rests on Benson, USMS and the coin with mint prefix CHRXoF. Effect: no ATH changes; 3 rug flags removed; max drawdown more than 5 points lower for 9 more coins; time above $100k +7,080 s (USMS) and +34,260 s (CHRXoF). The rugged sample is unchanged (20 of 20 keep the flag). What it might break: a real one-point crash (an hourly or daily bar) no longer lowers the level; with 1-minute data the delay is one minute.
5. **Do not rely on the $20B cap for the wick; apply the strict-window tests (volume at least $1,000, at least 5 wallets, liquidity at least $5,000) to the wick as well.** Rests on wigi ($19.7B in a minute of 4 swaps), PANDU ($70.8M, 34 times its ATH), Benson ($377M) and X ($67M with $26 traded). Not recomputed. What it might break: a genuine one-minute peak would no longer be reported as the wick.
6. **Do not widen `rugWithinSec` to 6 hours.** 115 more of 500 coins would be flagged (171 to 286); the 30-minute rule already flags all 20 coins of the rugged sample. The 115 were not inspected, so this rests on size alone.
7. **Do not use Dune's hourly `peak_p999_usd` as a pass/fail cross-check without a volume condition.** Of the 92 coins here, 41 are hourly flattening (3 to 133 times under ours, ours right) and 14 are dust prints (up to 8,400 times over, ours right); the hourly figure was right for 1 coin.

## 9. Spend and limits

- Codex.io: 491 compute units of 3,000. Every call was logged in a ledger outside the repo; a bars or events call costs about 4 to 5 units, a pair listing 4 plus 0.0048 per pair.
- Dune: 7.7 credits of 40 (two raw-trade queries, 3.805 and 3.923 credits by the usage counter: CLOUT on 2025-01-24 and CABARA on 2024-10-15). The Dune hourly figures on the list were already in hand.
- GeckoTerminal (public, no key, `User-Agent: ohlcv-check/1.0`, one call every 7 seconds, every response cached on disk outside the repo) and CoinGecko public: free. GeckoTerminal serves 180 days, so older coins were checked with Codex.io raw swaps and, for two coins, Dune raw trades.
- Codex.io raw swaps are the same vendor as the bars, a different endpoint; they are a weaker check than an independent source. For group 3 that is 28 of the 41 coins.
- USD volume computed from Codex.io events was unreliable (the coin is token0 in some pools; old events have null wallet and token addresses), so no verdict rests on it; verdicts use prices, wallet counts and swap counts.
- Verdict rules are in `ingest/scripts/a6/build-report.ts` (`verdictFor`); the eight group 6 coins and the Item 9 coins were judged by hand and are listed there with the reason.
